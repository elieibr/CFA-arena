# How to Pass the CFA Level 1 in 6 Months

Passing the CFA Level 1 is not about being the smartest person in the room. It is about consistency, the right study plan, and understanding what the exam actually tests. Six months is enough time — if you use it correctly.

## What the CFA Level 1 Actually Tests

The CFA Level 1 exam covers 10 topics across two sessions of 2 hours and 15 minutes each, with 90 multiple-choice questions per session. The pass rate historically sits between 35% and 45%, which means more than half of candidates fail — not because the material is impossible, but because they underestimate the volume and the style of questions.

The exam does not reward memorization. It rewards application. You need to read a scenario, identify what is being asked, and select the correct answer under time pressure. That is a skill you build through practice, not through reading textbooks.

## The 6-Month Study Plan

### Months 1–2: Build the Foundation

Start with the quantitative and conceptual topics that underpin everything else.

**Month 1**
- Week 1–2: Ethical and Professional Standards
- Week 3–4: Quantitative Methods (Time Value of Money, Statistics, Probability)

**Month 2**
- Week 1–2: Economics (Micro, Macro, International)
- Week 3–4: Financial Statement Analysis (Part 1: Income Statement, Balance Sheet)

At this stage, read the curriculum or a third-party provider summary, take notes on key formulas, and do at least 20 practice questions per topic before moving on.

### Months 3–4: Cover the Core Finance Topics

**Month 3**
- Week 1–2: Financial Statement Analysis (Part 2: Cash Flow, Ratios, Reporting Quality)
- Week 3–4: Corporate Issuers (Capital Budgeting, WACC, Capital Structure)

**Month 4**
- Week 1–2: Equity Investments (Valuation Models, Market Efficiency)
- Week 3–4: Fixed Income (Bond Valuation, Duration, Credit Analysis)

These are the heaviest topics in terms of calculation. Build formula sheets as you go. Do not skip practice questions.

### Month 5: Complete the Remaining Topics

- Week 1: Derivatives (Forwards, Futures, Options, Swaps)
- Week 2: Alternative Investments (Private Equity, Hedge Funds, Real Assets)
- Week 3: Portfolio Management (CAPM, Efficient Frontier, IPS)
- Week 4: Full review of Ethics — this topic alone can push you above or below the pass threshold

Ethics deserves a dedicated week at the end because it is scenario-based and the CFA Institute uses it as a differentiator when candidates are on the pass/fail boundary.

### Month 6: Exam Preparation

Stop reading new material. This month is entirely about practice and review.

- Week 1–2: Complete at least 2 full mock exams under timed conditions
- Week 3: Review every wrong answer. Understand why you were wrong, not just what the right answer was.
- Week 4: Light review of weak areas, formula consolidation, and rest

Do not study the night before the exam. Sleep matters more than one extra hour of review at that point.

## How Many Hours Do You Actually Need

The CFA Institute recommends 300 hours of study for Level 1. In a 6-month plan, that works out to roughly 12–13 hours per week. For most working professionals, that means 2 hours on weekday evenings and a 4–5 hour block on weekends.

If you can only commit 8–10 hours per week, extend your plan to 8 months. Do not compress the schedule and sacrifice practice time — that is where most candidates fail.

## The Most Important Thing Most Candidates Get Wrong

Reading without practicing is the single biggest mistake. The CFA Level 1 is a multiple-choice exam, and multiple-choice exams have a specific skill set: eliminating wrong answers, recognizing question patterns, and managing time.

You develop that skill by doing hundreds of practice questions — not by re-reading the same chapters. A good benchmark is 1,500 to 2,000 practice questions before exam day, with at least 3 full mock exams in the final month.

## Topic Weights and Where to Focus

| Topic | Exam Weight |
|---|---|
| Ethical and Professional Standards | 15–20% |
| Quantitative Methods | 6–9% |
| Economics | 6–9% |
| Financial Statement Analysis | 11–14% |
| Corporate Issuers | 6–9% |
| Equity Investments | 11–14% |
| Fixed Income | 11–14% |
| Derivatives | 5–8% |
| Alternative Investments | 7–10% |
| Portfolio Management | 8–12% |

Ethics is weighted highest for a reason. The CFA Institute takes professional conduct seriously and the exam reflects that. Do not treat it as an easy topic.

## Tools That Actually Help

- **Practice question banks** — volume matters more than the source
- **Formula sheets** — build your own as you study, do not just download someone else's
- **Mock exams** — timed, full sessions, no distractions
- **Active recall** — test yourself instead of re-reading notes

## Final Thoughts

Six months is enough. The candidates who fail are usually the ones who spent too much time reading and not enough time practicing, or who left Ethics until the last week and treated it like a simple review topic.

Start early, practice constantly, and treat mock exams as the real thing. The CFA Level 1 is a test of preparation, not intelligence.
